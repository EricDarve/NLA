# Iterative Methods for Eigenvalue Computation

The preceding chapter developed QR iteration for computing the full spectrum of a matrix. For very large matrices, however, storing and transforming the whole matrix can be too expensive. Often, an application needs only a few eigenvalues and their eigenvectors. This chapter develops methods designed for that task.

Many large matrices are **sparse**, meaning that most of their entries are zero. Multiplying such a matrix by a vector can be inexpensive, even when a dense eigenvalue computation is out of reach. The methods in this chapter use these matrix-vector products to build approximations without transforming the full matrix.

## The Main Idea

These methods collect directions generated by matrix-vector products into a **Krylov subspace**. Keeping this space small makes it possible to work with a matrix that would be too large for a full eigenvalue computation.

We represent the action of $A$ on this subspace by a small matrix. Its eigenvalues and eigenvectors provide approximations for selected eigenpairs of $A$. The QR methods from the preceding chapter are still useful: they solve this small eigenvalue problem.

The two main methods are **Arnoldi iteration** for general matrices and **Lanczos iteration** for symmetric matrices. Both build the subspace progressively, allowing us to improve the approximations as needed.

## Chapter Roadmap

The chapter develops these ideas in the following order:

- [Projection onto Krylov subspaces](krylov_subspaces.md) explains how to construct the small eigenvalue problem and relate its solutions to the original matrix.
